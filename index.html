Rider stability programme

This is an off-horse conditioning plan—not a riding lesson. Aim for comfortable, controlled movement and quality, not exhaustion.

15–20 minper session
5 daysshort sessions + 2 recovery days
Safety: use a clear floor, stable chair/wall for support, and tell a parent/guardian about your plan. Skip anything painful, dizzying, or unstable. No standing/kneeling on the exercise ball, and don't use weights on balance exercises.

Before you start

Warm-up (3 minutes): march gently 45 sec; shoulder circles 30 sec; hip circles 30 sec; ankle rocks 30 sec; easy sit-to-stands 45 sec.

Cool-down (2 minutes): slow breathing, gentle hip-flexor and chest stretch. No bouncing or forcing a stretch.

On each strength exercise, rest about 30–60 sec between sets. Stop a set when you can't keep the movement neat.

12-week check-ins 🌱

Build consistency gradually. This is a check-in—not a test or a promise that riding skills will automatically change.

How to progress

Weeks 1–2: learn the technique and use the lower end of the repetitions. Weeks 3–4: add 1–2 reps only if everything feels controlled. Weeks 5–6: keep the same exercises and focus on steadier breathing and symmetry. Weeks 7–8: progress a little only if the previous level is easy and pain-free. Weeks 9–12: maintain good form; ask a qualified coach/physio before adding harder or loaded variations.

You can repeat a week or take an easier week any time. There is no penalty for that.

Useful questions for my instructor

  • Am I sitting evenly left and right?
  • Can you check my pelvis and upper-body position?
  • What is one safe exercise to practise for my sitting trot?
  • Could you watch me do a simple balance exercise on the ground?

Research & why this plan looks like this

The British Horse Society says riders benefit from core strength, balance, flexibility and stamina, and recommends starting gradually. Research has found that structured core-stability programmes can improve riders' balance and trunk endurance; one small 8-week study reported improved left-right saddle-pressure symmetry. The evidence is promising but still limited—fitness supports riding, it doesn't replace instruction or guarantee a sitting canter.

Sources: British Horse Society: Rider fitness · 6-week core stability study · 8-week rider core programme study
